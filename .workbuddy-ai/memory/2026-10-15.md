## About section, second attempt: "rules becoming play" (`components/bench/`)

The v3 parallax handoff (`components/matter/`) was audited against the rendered
page and judged not good enough for the About — the practice-landing scene went
visually empty exactly when the reader arrived. Rather than retune it again, the
About's decorative layer was replaced with a new concept, chosen with the user:
audience **creative peers**, tone **playful and experimental**, separation
**completely new metaphor** (no DAW, waveform, signal or hardware).

### The concept

A ring of real constraints — the technologies and credits the biography already
names — is strewn across a sheet of graph paper. On scroll the ring contracts
and every chip lands on its slot; the thing they assemble around draws itself;
and the experiment steps aside for the one line of prose it was a demonstration
of. Signature `RULES → ASSEMBLY → PRINCIPLE`, published as `data-bench-state`
cycling `scatter → align → settle`.

### Decisions worth keeping

- **Depth is not a constant parallax ratio.** Each stratum spends its travel on
  its own beat: the sheet drifts 14px across the whole arc, the artifact 34px,
  and the chips' travel *is* their scatter, spent exactly at the dock. The
  ratio is large at the start and zero in the middle; the depth the reader reads
  comes from scale, opacity, paint order and scatter distance.
- **`benchRunway` takes a list of bands, not one.** The artifact is lower and
  expires earlier than the principle. Solving for the principle alone (the first
  version) left the panel climbing over the artifact while it was still drawing
  — measured at p=0.6 on the real page. Each band declares its own
  `clearUntil`; the largest solution wins.
- **The chip is two elements.** Outer = zero-size anchor at the slot, and the
  node the binder transforms. Inner = the label, centring itself with a static
  `transform`. One element cannot do both.
- **Every artifact stroke carries `pathLength="1"`**, so one normalised
  `stroke-dashoffset` draws the frame, dividers, plot and brackets together.
  Verified in-browser: `calc(1 - var(--bench-draw))` resolves correctly.
- **`translate: -50% -50%` does not survive GSAP.** GSAP folds the individual
  `translate`/`rotate`/`scale` properties into its own model the first time it
  touches a node, after which a declaration it did not write stops reaching
  paint. The artifact was measured sitting a half-width right of centre. The
  centring now lives inside the transform string the hook owns.
- **The scaffold.** The opening frame needed *shapes*, not just words: dashed
  bounds plus a centre mark, whole on the first frame and spent as the wireframe
  draws over them. It is a sibling of the drawing, not a group inside it, so its
  opacity does not multiply with `artifactOpacity` (which is 0 on frame one).

## About entry: retimed the arc, reshaped the seam

The user's report: the entry into About took too much scrolling, and the
transition into it did not read as smooth. Both turned out to be measurable, and
both had the same root — a phase table that did not finish until `p=0.80`.

**The numbers that were wrong (superseding the "Verified on the real page"
figures above).** At 1440×900 the section measured runway 2250px, hold 387px,
range 2582px. That is **3.30 viewports** of scroll before the reader reached a
word of the biography. The cause is that `benchRunway`'s solution carries a
`1 / (1 - clearUntil)` amplifier — the panel climbs the whole section's travel,
so a band that must stay clear until `p=0.80` costs five times its own height in
runway. Late phases are superlinearly expensive, which is why this is a budget
and not a taste.

**The seam was a 50/50 dissolve.** Screenshotted at `p=0.08` (opacity 0.552):
the rack's rail, lane rules, playhead, transport bar and mixer were all legible
*through* the graph paper, with the ten chips legible on top of them. Two dense
full-viewport interfaces blended at half contrast for 387px is not a smooth
fade — it is mush, and it was the whole of the perceived roughness.

**The fixes.**

- `BENCH_PHASES` retimed by the ratio of the two spans (`0.43 / 0.65`), keeping
  every internal relationship: gather `[0.12, 0.31]`, assemble `[0.17, 0.36]`,
  resolve `[0.33, 0.40]`, emerge `[0.43, 0.55]`. The hold fell from 0.15 to 0.12
  with it, since `BENCH_HOLD` is `gather[0]`.
- `BENCH_CHIP_STAGGER` rescaled `0.16 → 0.11`. It is a fraction of the **arc**,
  not of the gather window, so a retime silently changes its meaning: left alone
  it would have eaten three quarters of a window that is now only 0.19 wide.
- `BENCH_SEAM_FADE = 0.7` — the opacity finishes at 70% of the hold instead of
  100%, so the sheet is still travelling when it is fully opaque.
- `BENCH_SEAM_RISE = 64` px, scaled by `unit`, published as a new `stageY` field
  on `BenchFrame`. The sheet starts 72px below its resting place and rises into
  it, so the arrival has a leading edge. This is what actually kills the mush —
  the reader gets a spatial cue about which surface is arriving.
- The stage is not in the hook's `owned` list (GSAP must never touch it), so its
  transform is removed in `measure()` and in the teardown rather than restored
  from a snapshot. It happens to cancel in the band measurements — both are
  relative to the stage's own top edge — but `measure()` clears it anyway so the
  measurement does not depend on that invariant holding elsewhere.

**Measured after (1440×900).** Runway 1390, hold 207, range 1722. **Entry 1929px
= 2.14 viewports**, down from 3.30 — a third less scroll, with the artwork still
playing across 1722px. Seam: opacity completes 145px into the section with the
sheet still 15px from settling; the sheet lands exactly on the handoff boundary
at 207px. The both-visible window went from ~232px to ~83px.

Other viewports: 1920×1080 → 1.86vp, 1280×800 → 2.30vp, 1024×768 → 2.21vp.
390×844, 768×900 and both reduced-motion passes → `data-motion` absent, stage
`display: none`, runway 0. The hero's dive is unchanged at 2700px (hero height
3807 = 3600 + the 207px hold), which is the point of the hold contract: the
retime costs the reader scroll, not the hero its timing.

**Two new regression guards** in `tests/bench-frame.test.ts`, both with the
measured 1440×900 page written out as a fixture: the entry must stay under 2.4
viewports, and the artwork's range must stay above 1.5 viewports. The first pins
the user's complaint; the second pins the failure mode on the other side, which
is a section that snaps through its whole choreography in half a screen.

`tsc` clean, 398/398 across 17 suites, eslint clean, CSS purity 44 rules all
local. `app/layout.tsx:177` still reports `Illegal return statement` — unchanged
and still unfixed.

## The handoff, third pass: a disassembly, not a collapse

The user rejected the second pass. The complaint, verbatim: *"not the whole
scene about section that minimize but example header is go top, sidebar is go
left and etc so its seamless when zoom"*. So the seam is no longer the machine
shrinking away as one object — it is the machine coming apart, each piece along
the axis it already lives on.

**The shape.** `BENCH_HERO_COLLAPSE` (`scale: 0.72`, `radius: 26`, `fadeFrom`)
and the whole-stage `heroScale` / `heroOpacity` / `heroRadius` triple are gone.
In their place `BENCH_HERO_EXITS` — one row per piece, each with an offset as a
**percentage of its own box** and a `delay`:

| piece | exit | delay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `rail` | `x: -135%` | 0 |
| `proof` (mixer) | `y: 135%` | 0.06 |
| `playhead` | dissolves | 0.1 |
| `camera` (clip content) | dissolves | 0.14 |
| `chrome` (toolbar) | `y: -135%` | 0.18 |
| `ruler` | `y: -250%` | 0.24 |
| `ground` | dissolves | 0.34 |

Percentages and not px, deliberately: the stage clips, so a piece clears itself
by its own size at any viewport, and the table needs no measurement it cannot be
given. Verified by arithmetic that every piece fully leaves the clipped frame at
`BENCH_HOLD` (the rail is 130px wide and travels 175px; the ruler is 44px tall
and travels 110px, past the 46px toolbar above it). `heroCleared` replaced
`heroGone`; the frame now returns `heroExits[]` + `heroCleared`.

### Three findings that changed the design

1. **The camera cannot take an `opacity`.** `.camera` carries
   `transform-style: preserve-3d`, and `opacity < 1` is a *grouping property
   value* — it forces the element to render its subtree into a flat plane. The
   lanes' `translateZ` would vanish on the frame the fade began: at
   `IMMERSIVE_DEPTH_DIVE` the depth is 30px against a 1200px perspective, so the
   profile lane would snap ~2.5% smaller (≈5px vertically, ≈15px horizontally
   across the visible frame) at full opacity, mid-seam. So the exit table's
   `fade: boolean` became `mode: 'slide' | 'fade' | 'dissolve'`, and the camera's
   dissolve is delivered through a `--arr-fade` custom property on the camera
   node that the lanes multiply into their own `opacity`. The lanes are leaves
   with no 3D descendants, so their opacity can fall without flattening
   anything — the same reason `.laneProfile`'s existing `filter: brightness()`
   has always been safe.
2. **`.camera { will-change: opacity }` was a latent bug** in the motion block.
   Promoting a `preserve-3d` node is the documented way to flatten a 3D subtree
   — and the comment three lines above the rule already said the camera was left
   alone *because* of that. It would have killed the depth layer for the whole
   hero, not just the seam. Removed; the rule now matches its comment.
3. **`.lane`'s `opacity` transition can outlast the seam's whole window.** The
   120ms smoothing is the hero's (it hides a dropped frame across the dive's
   fifth of a 340svh section), but the dissolve is packed into the hold, so on a
   fast or slow scroll the timeline would still be visibly half-faded on the
   frame the stage parks its `visibility` — a pop, not a dissolve. The seam now
   sets `data-seam-locked` on the hero's stage for as long as its clock runs and
   `.stage[data-seam-locked] .lane { transition: filter 120ms linear }` drops
   only the opacity half. The `filter` half is kept: nothing about
   `--arr-profile-focus` changes while the seam runs, and dropping it would be a
   claim about the hero this rule has no business making.

Also: `.playhead` moved from `will-change: translate` to `will-change: opacity`
— it is the one piece that dissolves rather than slides. `data-seam-locked` and
not `data-seam`, because `docs/arrangement-hero.md` documents a retired
`data-seam="open|closed"` contract on `<section id="home">` and reusing the name
on the stage would read as current.
