# Landing page content audit 004

Scope: `features/landing-page/rack-01/rack-01.tsx` and `features/landing-page/constants/index.ts`.

Purpose: strengthen the portfolio as a sales page for frontend engineering work. This audit reviews clarity, proof, positioning, conversion, and AI-copy patterns. It does not change product code.

Mode: after implementation. Findings are reported without applying audit changes.

## Verdict

The page has a memorable point of view and several usable proof points: React, Next.js, TypeScript, four-plus years in frontend work, three product teams, a 15K+ user platform, a three-month launch, HRIS work, and production issue resolution.

The selling case is still buried under the music-console metaphor. The copy often names tools, surfaces, and signals while leaving the buyer to infer the value: what Aditya owns, what kind of product problem he solves, and what changed because he worked on it.

Priority order:

1. Fix project data trust issues.
2. Make the hero promise specific to the work a client or hiring team can request.
3. Turn experience and project descriptions into evidence-led case-study entries.
4. Reduce decorative system language where it competes with the sales message.

## Findings

### HIGH 1. Switchyard points to the Primarindo website

Source: `features/landing-page/constants/index.ts:225-231`.

`Switchyard` is described as a control plane for coding agents, but its URL is `https://primarindo.niqcode.com/`, the same URL used by `Primarindo Asia` at lines 237-243. The genre also says `Corporate / Manufacturing`, which conflicts with the description.

Why it hurts conversion: a prospective client who clicks the strongest technical project lands on an unrelated company profile. That makes the portfolio look unmaintained and weakens every other claim.

Fix: provide the real Switchyard URL and its real genre, or remove the project until the destination is ready. Confirm the image, title, description, URL, and genre as one record before publishing.

### HIGH 2. The page has proof, but the proof is not attached to outcomes

Sources: `rack-01.tsx:452-480`, `constants/index.ts:108-137`.

The hero says Aditya builds with React, Next.js, and TypeScript across products used by more than 15,000 people. The experience data adds a three-month launch and HRIS features. These are valuable facts, but they are spread across separate sections instead of forming a clear case.

Why it hurts conversion: the reader sees activity, not a compact reason to hire. The page should connect role, product problem, contribution, and result in the same sentence or card.

Recommended proof pattern:

`I led frontend work on Bisadaya, a job-seeker platform used by 15K+ people. I also launched Campus Connect in three months.`

Keep the sentence only if the ownership and numbers are accurate. Add one verified outcome for the HRIS work if one exists. Do not add a percentage without a source.

### MEDIUM 3. The hero headline describes a discipline, not a buying decision

Source: `rack-01.tsx:449-455`.

Current copy: `Production frontend, from architecture to interaction.`

The sentence is polished but broad. It does not tell a hiring manager or client what kind of help is available.

Stronger direction using existing evidence:

`I build the frontend systems people rely on after launch.`

Supporting line:

`React, Next.js, and TypeScript for production products, from reusable architecture to the details people use every day.`

This keeps the craft perspective while making production ownership clearer. If the target buyer is known, name them directly, such as product teams, SaaS teams, or founders.

### MEDIUM 4. The hero CTA is clear but not specific enough for high-intent visitors

Source: `rack-01.tsx:457-469`.

`Selected work` is a good navigation label, but it is not a strong next action. `Resume` serves hiring visitors but does not help a client decide whether to start a conversation.

Recommended CTA pair:

- Primary: `See the work`
- Secondary: `Read my experience` or `Open résumé`

If the site is primarily for freelance work, the primary CTA should instead be `Discuss a frontend project` and the work link should become secondary. The page currently tries to sell to hiring teams and clients at the same time without choosing which conversion matters most.

### MEDIUM 5. The Profile section repeats the technology inventory three times

Source: `rack-01.tsx:509-541`.

The three profile cards cover frontend technologies, architecture, and infrastructure. The body copy remains mostly a list of tools: React, Next.js, TypeScript, SSR, Zustand, React Query, Docker, Nginx, and monitoring.

Why it hurts conversion: skill lists prove familiarity, but they do not prove judgment. A buyer needs to know what decisions Aditya makes with those tools.

Recommended content shape for each card:

- Situation: what product or team problem appeared.
- Contribution: what Aditya owned.
- Result: what changed, using a real metric where available.

Example based on existing facts:

`I structure React and Next.js features so teams can keep shipping without duplicating UI or state logic.`

This is a positioning statement, not a measured claim. Pair it with a real project link or a concrete example before presenting it as proof.

### MEDIUM 6. The Profile headline is memorable but does not identify the buyer value

Source: `rack-01.tsx:603-604`.

Current copy: `I build the layer people actually use.`

This has voice and should not be removed. It needs a second line that explains the commercial value.

Suggested supporting line:

`I turn product requirements into frontend systems that stay usable as the product grows.`

The existing cards can then prove this sentence. The headline gives personality, while the supporting line gives the hiring reason.

### MEDIUM 7. The Skills controller over-prioritizes interaction over proof

Sources: `rack-01.tsx:1313-1511`, `constants/index.ts:183-219`.

The section says `The stack behind the shipped work`, but every skill is initialized at level `100`. The control labels and synthesized audio create a strong experience, yet the page does not show which skill mattered on which shipped product.

Why it hurts conversion: a 100% fader for every technology reads as an ungrounded proficiency claim. It also makes React, Grafana, and Uptime Kuma appear equally important.

Fix direction:

- Keep the controller as the visual signature.
- Change the section support copy to explain that the controls map to real project work.
- Add project or use-case associations to the data, for example `React: Bisadaya, Campus Connect`.
- Remove universal 100% levels unless the numbers have a defined meaning.

Suggested support copy:

`Explore the tools behind the projects below. Each bank points to a part of the work I have shipped or supported.`

### MEDIUM 8. Some experience bullets use vague performance language

Source: `constants/index.ts:108-137`.

Strong bullets:

- `Led frontend development for Bisadaya, a job-seeker platform serving 15K+ users.`
- `Built and launched Campus Connect within three months.`

Weak or incomplete bullets:

- `Developed SaaS HRIS features and KPI automation for HR teams.`
- `Resolved critical production issues and optimized performance across HR workflows.`
- `Built frontend features for an HR management system with tRPC and Prisma services.`

The weaker bullets name responsibilities but omit scale, ownership, or result. Replace them with the exact change made and one verified effect. Useful placeholders while gathering evidence:

`Built [feature] for [user/team], reducing [real measure] from [real baseline] to [real result].`

`Resolved [named production issue] across [workflow], restoring [real user or operational outcome].`

Do not publish the bracketed form. It is a data request, not final copy.

### MEDIUM 9. The Experience section treats education as a job record

Source: `constants/index.ts:142-176`.

The `Education` group sits beside product roles in the same radio and cassette system. The GPA and lab assistant detail are useful proof, but the section does not visually or verbally distinguish education from paid product work.

Why it hurts conversion: it can make the timeline harder to scan and slightly dilutes the four-plus years of professional experience.

Fix: label it `Education and training`, move it after the product roles, and keep only the credentials that strengthen the desired role. The lab assistant detail is more persuasive than a generic course label because it shows responsibility.

### MEDIUM 10. Project descriptions are mostly category labels, not case-study hooks

Source: `constants/index.ts:226-307`.

`Habit Tracker`, `SeaPhantom`, `SeaPhantom P2P`, and `Labgrownbeasts` are described in one sentence each. The descriptions say what each thing is, but not what Aditya solved, built, or improved.

Recommended minimum structure for each project:

`Built [product type] for [user or business need]. Owned [specific frontend contribution].`

For Habit Tracker, the current description can become more useful without inventing facts:

`A habit tracking app for daily routines. The interface covers streaks and weekly overviews in one focused flow.`

This still needs a contribution sentence, such as the actual architecture, interaction, or deployment work, before it becomes a strong sales proof point.

### MEDIUM 11. Primarindo Asia is clear about the subject but weak about the contribution

Source: `constants/index.ts:237-245`.

The description explains the company and its manufacturing business, but it reads like a company summary. It does not explain what the portfolio owner built.

The project entry should answer:

- What information or conversion problem did the site need to solve?
- What did Aditya own in the frontend?
- What interaction, content system, or responsive behavior made the result work?

Keep the company facts if they are accurate, but lead with the product work. A portfolio project should describe the work performed, not only the client.

### MEDIUM 12. Technical language sometimes replaces plain language

Examples: `control plane`, `signal path`, `carrier`, `stack`, `synthesizer`, `arrangement workstation`, `field radio`, and `open channel`.

These terms create a distinctive identity, so they are not automatically a problem. The issue is density. When every section uses the metaphor, the reader has to decode the page before understanding the offer.

Recommendation: keep the music vocabulary in labels, transitions, and one or two interaction moments. Use plain language for the sentences that sell capability, experience, and outcomes.

### LOW 13. Several decorative lines make broad claims without adding evidence

Sources: `rack-01.tsx:56-61`, `rack-01.tsx:1626-1627`, `rack-01.tsx:2565`.

Examples include `Production interfaces, tuned with intent`, `Signal in, clear decisions out`, `Turning capability into experience`, and `Rotating portfolio footer signal`.

These lines fit the visual world, but they are not persuasive proof. Keep one as atmosphere if desired. Replace the others with useful orientation or a specific action, especially near the work and contact sections.

### LOW 14. Contact copy is strong in tone but vague in scope

Source: `rack-01.tsx:2396-2402`.

Current copy: `Bring me the hard frontend. I work where product logic, performance, and interface behavior meet. If that part of your product needs an owner, start here.`

The headline has a clear voice. The paragraph should tell the visitor what to contact Aditya about.

Suggested version:

`I can help with a new frontend build, a product interface that needs structure, or an existing React application that needs careful improvement.`

This lists real service shapes without inventing a client type or outcome. Replace `can help` with a narrower offer if the business focus is known.

### LOW 15. Contact pads mix conversion actions with audio toys

Source: `rack-01.tsx:2175-2200`.

Email, LinkedIn, GitHub, and Resume are useful conversion paths. Kick, Snare, Chord, Tone, Sub, Rim, Fifth, Pluck, Bass, Hat, Minor, and Bell are interaction features.

Why it matters: visitors who arrive ready to contact you must find the professional actions without scanning sixteen pads. Keep the audio bank, but visually group the four real contact actions and label them as `Contact`, `Profile`, `Code`, and `Résumé`.

### LOW 16. Project data has a numbering gap and dead commented content

Source: `constants/index.ts:261-271`, plus project IDs 0, 1, 2, and 4.

The commented `Frontend Resources` record and the missing visible ID 3 are maintenance signals. The UI displays a count based on the active array, but the data still carries an old sequence.

Fix: remove retired content from the constant file or restore it with a verified URL. Renumber IDs only if IDs are not used for persisted state or external references.

## Copy system recommended for the next pass

Use this order for each important content block:

1. What Aditya builds.
2. Who or what it helped.
3. What he owned.
4. What changed, with a verified measure where available.
5. Where the visitor can inspect the work.

The current page already contains enough source material for a stronger first pass. The most valuable verified facts are:

- React, Next.js, and TypeScript frontend work.
- Four-plus years in frontend engineering.
- Three product teams represented in the experience data.
- Bisadaya serving 15K+ users.
- Campus Connect launched within three months.
- SaaS HRIS features and KPI automation.
- Production HR workflow issue resolution.
- Backend integrations, Docker, Nginx, and self-hosted monitoring, if these are accurate and relevant to the target buyer.

## Proposed content hierarchy

Hero: one clear offer, one proof line, one work CTA.

Profile: a short point of view, followed by three evidence-led capabilities.

Skills: show tools through project associations rather than equal proficiency meters.

Experience: lead with shipped product work, then show role and timeline.

Selected work: use project-specific contribution and outcome copy, not only product category.

Contact: state the problems Aditya is available to own and provide one primary contact action.

## Audit checklist

- [ ] Confirm the real Switchyard URL and genre.
- [ ] Decide whether the primary buyer is a hiring team, a freelance client, or both.
- [ ] Add one verified outcome to each major experience entry.
- [ ] Add Aditya's contribution to every selected project description.
- [ ] Define what skill levels mean or remove the 100% proficiency presentation.
- [ ] Separate professional experience from education.
- [ ] Group professional contact actions apart from sound pads.
- [ ] Keep the music metaphor in the interface layer, not every important sales sentence.
- [ ] Re-run the copy audit after the missing facts are supplied.

## Verdict

The rack is distinctive enough to earn attention. The next pass should make the content easier to trust and easier to buy: fix the broken project destination, choose the buyer, then connect every major claim to ownership and evidence.
